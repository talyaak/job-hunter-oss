# Method notes

## Principles
1. **Truth over exaggeration**: presentation can improve, facts cannot change.
2. **Every CV targets a role family**: there is no universal CV. Keep a master CV and derive variants.
3. **Evidence beats buzzwords**: show leadership, ownership or problem solving through projects and results.
4. **Results over duties**: translate responsibilities into impact (time, cost, quality, adoption, scale).
5. **ATS awareness without promises**: natural, accurate terminology; simple formatting; no guaranteed ranking.
6. **Transferable skills count** when they are real and phrased truthfully.
7. **Recruiter-friendly**: who is this person, why relevant, what value, in the first few lines.
8. **Ask, do not assume**: never infer a responsibility just because the job description lists it.

## Fit dimensions (default weights; adjustable per job)
| Dimension | What it examines | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| must_haves | share of essential requirements met | 35 |
| keywords | relevant skills and terminology present with evidence | 15 |
| experience | requested level and years vs. documented | 20 |
| tools | hands-on use of the required tools | 20 |
| domain | closeness of past work to the target domain | 10 |

Scoring guide per dimension: 0-20 absent, 21-40 mostly missing, 41-60 partial, 61-80 solid, 81-100 strong direct evidence.
Count "partial" as partial; do not round it up because the topic feels similar.

## Gap-bridging questions (generate from the actual gaps)
- Which parts of that technology did you actually use, in what environment, for how long?
- Who owned design vs. operation? What broke, and how did you find out?
- How was success measured (before/after numbers, adoption, incidents)?
- Was it autonomous or deterministic? Were there approvals, validation, retries, audit?
- Did you do comparable work under another name or tool?
- Anything before your current role that is relevant (projects, service, freelance)?

## Typical weak spots
Skills inventories without proof; management title hiding hands-on depth; a single-industry label; tenure below the
requested years; vague "improved efficiency"; missing scale (people, teams, items); duplicate skills in several
categories; long skills lists where only a few are defensible.
